The app reads the PC once and shares the result with every widget that asked for it. A widget lists the sources it reads in its manifest, under `permissions.sources`, then binds them in its view (`"bind": "@gpu/percent"`) or reads them in its collector (`ctx.sources.gpu.percent`).

A source is read only while a widget on screen uses it, at the fastest rate any of them asks for.

The CPU, memory, GPU, network and media sources are what the native helper already reads for the built-in widgets. The rest are planned.

## Sources that ask first

Media, processes and the active window say something about what you're doing, so installing a widget that reads them asks you first. A widget that reads one of them and can also reach the network, or an app on your PC, could send what it reads off your PC: it gets a stronger prompt, and always a human review.

## Never offered

To any widget, ever: your files, your clipboard, your keystrokes, what's on your screen, running commands, and other widgets' secrets.

## Temperatures

GPU temperatures come from the vendors' own interfaces. Reading a CPU's temperature on Windows needs a kernel driver, which ShiftPane won't ship; for now that comes from apps that have one, like LibreHardwareMonitor, through a [local permission](/docs/permissions#apps-on-your-pc-or-network). The [Temperatures](/widgets/hardware-temps) widget does exactly that.
