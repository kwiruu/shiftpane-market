A collector is code from someone else running on your PC, so a widget may touch only what its manifest declares, you see that in plain words before it runs, and the gallery reviews it before it's listed.

## What a widget can declare

| Permission | In the manifest | What installing says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System sources | `"sources": ["gpu"]` | Reads your GPU load |
| Actions | `"actions": ["media.next"]` | Skips to the next track |
| Internet hosts | `"network": ["api.github.com"]` | Can reach api.github.com |
| Secrets | `"secrets": ["GITHUB_TOKEN"]` | Needs your GITHUB_TOKEN |
| Apps on your PC or network | `"local": ["127.0.0.1:8085"]` | Can reach 127.0.0.1:8085 on your PC |

## When installing asks

Installing shows everything a widget can do, in words like the ones above. Some things ask before the widget runs at all:

- Sources that say what you're doing: what's playing, your busiest programs, the window you're in.
- Any app on your PC or network.

An update that adds any permission asks again. One that doesn't is applied without a prompt.

## Apps on your PC or network

`permissions.local` names an exact host and port, such as `127.0.0.1:8085` for LibreHardwareMonitor's web server, or `192.168.1.20:8123` for Home Assistant. It's how widgets get data the app doesn't provide, without the app growing a source for every program. It always asks, and it's always flagged for review.

## The flow rule

Data can always flow from sources to your phone. But a widget that reads a source that says what you're doing, and can also reach a host, could send that off your PC. It gets a stronger prompt, and a person always reviews it.

## Secrets go to their issuer

A widget that declares `GITHUB_TOKEN` may only declare hosts that belong to GitHub. A token and an unrelated host is the shape of a widget that sends your token somewhere, so review refuses it.

## How review works

Every upload goes through automated checks at once. A failed check blocks the upload; a flag goes to the reviewer.

Then a person reviews every new widget, and every version that changes its permissions, with the report, the permissions, a diff against the last version, the collector's source, and a preview from `sample.json` in front of them.

The fast lane skips that person for an update that asks for nothing the approved version didn't, when it has no collector, or its author is verified or has three approved versions and none yanked. A first version, and any update that asks for more, always waits for a person.

## Signed, and revocable

- The gallery signs its index with an Ed25519 key, and each version carries its package's SHA-256. The app refuses a package whose hash or signature doesn't check out.
- A widget that turns out to be harmful is yanked. It lands on a signed revocation list the app reads at start and every six hours, and the app turns that version off, saying why.
